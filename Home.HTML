What is a fish?

A fish is an aquatic vertebrate animal that breathes using gills, has fins for movement, and typically has a long, scaly body. They are cold-blooded, meaning their body temperature changes with the water, and they live in both freshwater and saltwater environments.

Why are they so great?

Fish are vital to the environment because they are a crucial part of the food web, act as nutrient recyclers,and help maintain habitats. They control populations of their prey, provide food for predators like seabirds and marine mammals,and their waste recycles essential nutrients that support primary producers like algae and phytoplankton.

How do you distinguish a species of fish?

You can distinguish a fish by examining its body shape, fins, and coloration, and by observing its behavior and habitat. Key features for identification include the number, shape, and position of fins (dorsal, caudal, pectoral, pelvic, anal), mouth shape and size, eye characteristics, and any distinctive markings like spots or stripes.
